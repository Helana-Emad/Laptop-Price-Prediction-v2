Types of laptops

Each type is built for a different job. Here is what sets them apart.

Ultrabook

Thin, light, long battery life. Good for students and people who carry their laptop all day.

Trade-off: weak graphics, harder to upgrade.

Gaming

Powerful processor and a dedicated graphics card, with a fast screen and strong cooling.

Trade-off: heavy, loud, short battery life.

Workstation / Creator

Made for 3D, CAD, video editing and design. Accurate screen and a strong GPU.

Trade-off: expensive and bulky.

Business

Durable body, good keyboard, security features and reliable battery. Built for daily office work.

Trade-off: limited graphics power.

2-in-1

Touch screen that folds or detaches, so it works as a laptop or a tablet. Great for notes and drawing.

Trade-off: usually costs more for the same power.

Chromebook

Simple, fast and cheap. Runs ChromeOS and works best with web apps and Google tools.

Trade-off: no full Windows programs.

Specs explained

CPU (processor)
The brain of the laptop. Core i3 or Ryzen 3 for light use, i5 or Ryzen 5 for most people, i7/Ryzen 7 and above for heavy work.
RAM
How many things the laptop can handle at once. 8 GB is the minimum today, 16 GB is the comfortable choice.
SSD vs HDD
An SSD is much faster and quieter than an HDD. Always choose an SSD for the system drive.
GPU (graphics)
Integrated graphics are enough for study and office work. A dedicated GPU (such as NVIDIA RTX) is needed for gaming, 3D and video editing.
Screen
Choose Full HD at least. 14 inches is easy to carry, 15.6 inches gives more space. An IPS panel gives better colors and viewing angles.
Battery
Look for real-world use of 8 hours or more if you study or work away from a charger.
